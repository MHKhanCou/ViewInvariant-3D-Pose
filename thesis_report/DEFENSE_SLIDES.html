06
Method

Why the camera rotation cancels

In words: the frame is built out of the body itself. Move the camera and the whole prediction turns — but the frame, being made of the same joints, turns by exactly the same amount. Measuring the pose in that frame divides the turn out on both sides.

P the predicted skeleton
R the body frame built from it
Q the unknown rotation between the two cameras
⊤ transpose — for a rotation, its inverse
P(B) = P(A)Q   so   R(B) = Q⊤R(A)
P(B)R(B) = (P(A)Q)(Q⊤R(A)) = P(A)R(A)  since QQ⊤ = I
Q is never estimated. It meets its own inverse and disappears — which is why no calibration is required.

Boundary

The contribution is a boundary, tested at three levels

Choosing between constructionslonger shoulder axis wins, 5.2% / 4.4%, both backbonesHOLDS
Which frame to trustthe multi-scale gain was circular by constructionFAILS
Which joint disagreesslope 0.218 against a predicted band of [0.038, 0.073]FAILS
Joints rigid with the torso sit at a larger radius than articulated joints, yet disagree two and a half times less — the opposite of the rigid-segment prediction. A human is not a rigid body.

The principle holds at the construction level and does not extend reliably to the finer two.

12
Boundary

So what is actually new?

Not new, and all of it cited: the TRIAD construction (Black, 1964), the primary-axis rule (Shuster & Oh), the 2σ⁄L error propagation from biomechanics, Kabsch alignment.

The transferDoes classical rigid-body geometry survive on joints inferred by a network?
The boundaryIt survives at construction level; it fails at both finer levels
The evidence372.7 → 93.4 mm, 179 of 180 pairs, replicated on a second backbone
The negatives17 pre-registered experiments; more than half failed; all reported
The contribution is the experimentally established boundary of the geometric approach — not the invention of the geometry.
13
Limits

Limitations — stated, not defended away

  • The geometry predates this thesis by decades. The contribution is the transfer and its boundary.
  • Cross-view distance measures agreement, not correctness. Two predictions wrong the same way agree perfectly.
  • No downstream task succeeded — cross-view retrieval recall fell, and that claim is withdrawn.
  • n = 2 datasets, both laboratory rigs, and 2 backbones, both temporal transformers. Replication, not universality.

Each of these is stated in the report, not only here.

What exactly the Kabsch baseline is

For each predicted pose independently, find the rotation that minimises squared distance to one fixed reference skeleton, then apply it. Closed form via SVD — no training, no labels, no calibration, one view.

Robustness testedResult
Three unrelated reference skeletonsbaseline wins under all three
Every centring convention testedbaseline wins under all
Reference scaled to child proportionsmoves 0.12 mm — no matched template needed
All fifteen Human3.6M actionsbaseline wins 15 / 15
Why it wins: it fits one rotation over all seventeen joints, so no single joint dominates. My frame reads four, so those four carry the whole orientation — which is also why the two fail on different joints.
