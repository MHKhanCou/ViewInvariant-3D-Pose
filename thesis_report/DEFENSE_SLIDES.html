The problem in the proposal

A monocular estimator predicts the skeleton in the observing camera's frame. Two cameras, same person, same instant → two different sets of numbers.

Gait analysis across sessions, sports across camera positions — all need one shared frame first.

2

What I built — one step, after a frozen model

Steps 1–3 are frozen and unmodified. Step 4 is mine: rebuild the coordinate frame from the person's own hips and torso. Zero trained parameters, 402 FLOPs.

3

Why the camera rotation cancels

Both axes are read from the joints, so the frame rotates with the body. If camera B sees the same pose under an unknown rotation Q:

P(B) = P(A)Q   and   R(B) = Q⊤R(A)
P(B)R(B) = (P(A)Q)(Q⊤R(A)) = P(A)R(A)
Q is never estimated — it cancels. That is why no calibration is needed.

Exact only if both cameras give the same pose up to rotation. In practice each carries its own error, and the residual distance we measure is that error.

8

Four ideas I started with did not survive

  • Novel canonicalization → it is TRIAD (Black, 1964). Prior art, cited.
  • Reliability predicts pose error → falsified five independent ways.
  • Bone-length predicts error → ρ = +0.492 on one dataset, +0.098 on the other. Retracted.
  • Multi-scale per-limb frames → scored on the joints they were built from. Demoted.
One shared cause: geometric plausibility is invariant to coherent depth error. A pose wrong only in depth is still symmetric, proportioned and well conditioned — geometry cannot see the error, because the error is what geometry preserves.

I found each of these myself, and reported them rather than removing them.

10

So what is actually new?

Not new — and all of it cited: the TRIAD construction (Black, 1964), the primary-axis rule (Shuster & Oh), the 2σ/L error propagation (biomechanics), Kabsch alignment.

1. The transfer Does classical rigid-body geometry survive on joints inferred by a network?
2. The boundary It survives at construction level and fails at the two finer levels
3. The evidence 372.7 → 93.4 mm, 179/180, replicated on a second backbone
4. The negatives 17 pre-registered experiments, more than half failed, all reported
The contribution is the experimentally established boundary of the geometric approach — not the invention of the geometry.
11

Limitations — stated, not defended away

  • The geometry predates this thesis by decades. The contribution is the transfer and its boundary.
  • Cross-view distance measures agreement, not correctness. Two predictions wrong the same way agree perfectly.
  • No downstream task succeeded — cross-view retrieval recall fell, and that claim is withdrawn.
  • n = 2 datasets and 2 backbones. Replication, not universality.
17 experiments pre-registered with criteria committed to git before each run. More than half failed. 304 numerical claims re-derived from stored artifacts by an automated audit; 76 tests; every pre-registration verified to precede its own result.
